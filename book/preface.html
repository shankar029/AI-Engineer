Preface

Understand the operations. Then make better engineering decisions.

Why this book starts with foundations

Building an AI feature can look simple: send text to a model and display the result. The difficult questions appear soon afterward. What information did the model receive? Why did an input use so many tokens? What can a similarity score tell us? Which part of a memory estimate belongs to the model, and which part belongs to the application?

This book builds the tools for answering those questions. It starts with numbers and representations, follows how learning changes parameters, and then traces how a Transformer combines context. Each chapter asks you to calculate something small enough to check, rather than memorize a component name.

A foundation for a wider engineering practice

AI engineering also involves data choices, evaluation, retrieval, application design, permissions, deployment and operational care. Understanding a model does not replace those responsibilities. It helps you separate what the model calculates from what the surrounding system must guarantee.

These chapters develop mathematical and model foundations. Wider systems topics are connections and next steps, not additional lessons already taught here.

Learn through a sequence you can reproduce

A definition is useful only when you can use it. We move from an intuitive question to a visible example, then name the operation and calculate its result. Symbols are explained where they enter the lesson. Exercises and interview questions include reasoning, not just an answer key.

Small examples deliberately leave out much of a trained model. Their purpose is to expose the mechanics and the assumptions. A successful calculation is evidence that you followed the operation; it is not evidence that a real system is accurate, safe or ready for production.

Use the book as a working notebook. Predict an output, write down the intermediate values, and compare your result with the explanation. A mistake in a tiny example is inexpensive and often reveals exactly which idea needs another pass.
